MY LEADERSHIP JOURNEY

A1 Youth of Sorsogon

One of the most meaningful experiences in my life was being selected as one of the 150 youth participants of A1 Youth of Sorsogon, a youth development program initiated by our governor. Out of thousands of applicants from different municipalities, I was fortunate enough to be chosen as one of them.

My journey started when I was in Grade 9 and continued for almost three years, until I graduated from the program in October 2025 as a Grade 12 student. Our training camps were held every weekend, from Friday to Sunday, where we developed our leadership, communication, and community involvement skills.

Throughout the program, I was entrusted with a leadership role that involved leading the A1 participants, including Batch 2. It was a responsibility I genuinely valued because it allowed me to work with different people, communicate with fellow youth, and become more confident in taking initiative.

During the commencement exercise, I was also recognized as a CommuniLeader Awardee. Receiving this recognition made the experience even more meaningful and reminded me of how much I had grown throughout the program.

And honestly? I think I enjoy leadership more than studying sometimes. I love organizing, taking initiative, working with people, and being part of something that makes a difference. Of course, my academics still matter, but leadership is something I genuinely look forward to.

A1 Youth taught me that leadership is not simply about being in charge. It is about serving others, listening, understanding people, and showing up when others need you. It strengthened my passion for community service and made me appreciate what young people can accomplish when they choose to get involved.

08 — LOOKING BACK

A little reflection.

Making this webpage was a fun learning experience, although it took more patience than I expected. There were times when the layout wouldn't look right, and I'd have to go back to my code to figure out what went wrong. Sometimes, even a small mistake—like a missing closing tag or an uneven grid column—could affect an entire section.

Through building this site, I learned that I enjoy the design and structure side of coding just as much as logic. I improved my layout spacing, fixed mobile responsiveness across smaller screen sizes, and practiced using semantic HTML elements, CSS Grid, Flexbox, and hover effects. It was rewarding to turn a class requirement into something that actually feels personal.
