My Path into Computing

Ever since I was a child I was always interested in computers. For as long as I can remember, I have had a computer. I spent my time by randomly tweaking with it, looking into it, and have fun with it. Eventually, it became part of my identity. When the people in my life think about a computer, they usually think about me with it.

After that, during my times in elementary, it was just drilled onto my brain that I would either pursue Computer Engineering or Computer Science. Eventually, when the pandemic came around, I saw all of this content regarding computer science and just made up my mind. I was going to be a computer science student.

And now, as a computer science student, I finally have the opportunity to pursue this interest as a career. Although, I may not know where this path will take me, I shall keep striving forward. I code at a time.

Reflection

When first making this website, I couldn’t decide on a theme. I just didn’t know what theme could possibly represent me as a person. That’s when I had the idea: who else could know me better than my friends? They do say that close friends may see certain sides of you that even you wouldn’t realize. So that’s what I did. I asked a friend what theme I should go with and eventually came up with the cyberpunk theme. To explain it, I used to really love the Cyberpunk anime, and although I haven’t played the game, I’m a really big gamer. Plus, I’m in tech so I guess I just thought it fit me. It encompassed three parts of me, my old love for anime, my passion for gaming, and my identity as a tech bro. I gave my all for the first design expecting to have a lighter work load for when the final project came along. With this, I became complacent. Without realizing it, the instructions for the final project website also contained changes to the original template. There came new sections like the path to computing, favorites and skills, and this reflection I’m writing now. Because I didn’t check right away, I rushed those additions. From this, I realized that to make a good webpage, one needs to give it time and effort, and a clear vision of what one intends to do with it. Without them, the page becomes cluttered, scattered, and inconsistent. It wouldn’t be able to perform its due purpose. Compared to my About Me activity, my website now supports mobile view, new sections have been added, and new information based on the requirements given. Most of all, I learned that feeling prepared is not a reason to stop paying attention, and that I should read updated instructions as soon as they are posted instead of cramming.
